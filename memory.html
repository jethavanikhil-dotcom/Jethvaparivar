Chapter 2

Our gotra: Vasishtha

આપણું ગોત્ર: વસિષ્ઠ

The sage whose star still shines over our family.

A gotra is a family lineage passed down from father to children, named after the rishi from whom the line is said to descend. Our Kuldev tells us which goddess protects our family. Our gotra tells us which sage our line honours as its first teacher.

Maharishi Vasishtha, “the most excellent”, is counted among the Saptarishi, the seven great sages. He is the chief author of the seventh book of the Rigveda, and was the guru of Shri Rama and his brothers; the Yoga Vasishtha is written as a conversation between them. His wife Arundhati is honoured as the ideal of devotion and faithfulness. He is remembered for patience, forgiveness and wisdom.[10]

Vasishtha and Arundhati in the night sky

The seven bright stars of the Great Bear are the seven sages. Vasishtha is the star in the middle of the handle (Mizar), and close beside him shines the small star Arundhati (Alcor). At many Hindu weddings the couple are shown the Arundhati star, as a blessing for a marriage as steady as theirs.

In puja, the priest names the family’s gotra in the sankalp: “Vasishtha gotra”. In marriage Tradition, people of the same gotra are seen as brothers and sisters, so families have traditionally avoided marriages within the same gotra.

Documented A printed gotra table, “Aapna Gotraj: Our Family Line”, with information from our Barot, lists Jethwa · Vasishtha Rishi. See it in the archive.

Chapter 3

Our Kuldev: Chorwad Mata

આપણા કુળદેવ: ચોરવાડ માતા

The Mother who watches over our line, wherever we live.

A family’s Kuldev stays the same wherever the family lives. A Jethwa family in London, Nairobi or Jamnagar still has the same Kuldev as its ancestors, and she is the first to be remembered at important moments in family life.

Chorwad Mata is a form of Bhavani, the Mother Goddess. Her best-known temple is at Chorwad, on the coast of Junagadh district, where she is also called Zund Bhavani. Tradition A merchant sheltering from a storm made an offering to the goddess. She promised to appear on her own, asking that the temple doors stay closed until four hours after sunrise. The doors were opened too early, and only her face had formed. That is why she is worshipped there as a face alone.

Our shrine in Khambhalia

Our family’s ancestral shrine is in Khambhalia, in Devbhumi Dwarka district. As you face the shrines, Chorwad Mata is on the left and Sikotra Mata on the right. This is where the Jethwa family goes to do nivad.

Protection

She watches over the family line, its health, its children and its home.

New beginnings

Weddings and births are offered to her first, so they start with her blessing.

Belonging

Returning to the shrine our grandparents visited ties each generation to the ones before.

Guardian of the shrine

Kantaben, our Baa, wife of Gunvantray Bapa, has cared for the shrine of our Kuldev in Khambhalia for many years. When families come for nivad, from nearby or from abroad, it is Baa who welcomes them. Her care keeps our Kuldev’s home ready for every generation, and we are deeply grateful to her.

The nivad feast · નિવેદ

The day the whole family comes home

Fires are lit before dawn, the smell of ghee fills the air, lapsi and ladva are made by many hands, and relatives who have travelled from near and far embrace again. Every dish is first offered to Mataji, then shared as prasad. Every dish is cooked pure, without garlic or onion.

Chapter 4

Sikotra Mata

સિકોતર માતા

Goddess of the sea, and of every safe return home.

Beside our Kuldev in Khambhalia stands the shrine of Sikotra Mata, on the right as you face the shrines. Also called Sikotar Mata or Vahanvati Mata, she is a goddess of the western coast of Gujarat. For centuries sailors, fishermen and traders from Saurashtra prayed to her before going to sea and gave thanks when a voyage came home safely. Her old images often show her seated on a boat. Temples to her still stand along the coast, at Ralej near Khambhat, Dholera and Dahej.

Her place beside Chorwad Mata reflects the coastal world of our ancestors, where the sea shaped both livelihood and faith.

Chapter 5

Our Sura Pura Bapa

આપણા સુરાપુરા બાપા

A humble name. A hero’s heart.

Documented The stone plaque on the wall beside the shrine in Morzar reads જેઠવા પરિવારના સુરાપુરા કચરા બાપા: “Surapura of the Jethwa family, Kachra Bapa.”

A Surapura (from shur, brave) is an ancestor who gave his life protecting others: his village, his family’s women, the cattle, or those who came to him for shelter. Across Saurashtra and Kutch such heroes were remembered with a memorial stone called a paliya, and over generations their descendants came to worship them as protectors of the family line.[11]

Tradition In everyday Gujarati, kachro means something of no value. Families sometimes gave a cherished child a deliberately humble name to turn away the evil eye. This may explain Kachra Bapa’s name, but we have no record of why he was given it.

Unresolved

We do not yet know when Kachra Bapa lived, or where he fits in Dewji Bapa’s tree. If your elders or the Barot know, please tell us.

Chapter 6

Our Barot

આપણા બારોટ

The keepers of our names.

For centuries, Barot families kept handwritten books (vahi) recording the births, marriages and generations of the families they served. They would visit each family and read its line aloud, adding the newest generation. Many family trees in Saurashtra can be traced back further through the Barot’s books than through any other record.

Family record Our Barot: Bhanu, Bhagvi and Ranchod. Places recorded with them: Vadodra, Sultanpur, Golvada and Nyaymandir Pacchad.

Documented The note on the paper tree and the printed gotra record name the Barot as Shri Bhanuprasad Bhagvatiray.

If you are in touch with the Barot, ask them to check the names in the family tree against their books, especially the earliest generations and the names still to confirm.
