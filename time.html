Time · Chapter 1

The Jethwas of Saurashtra

સૌરાષ્ટ્રના જેઠવા

From the Barda hills to the throne of Porbandar. The Jethwa name is one of the oldest clan names in Saurashtra, carried with honour for more than nine centuries.

Origins

A clan of the Barda hills

Tradition

The Jethwas are one of the old ruling clans of western Saurashtra. Their traditional histories, kept by bards over many centuries, trace the clan back to a founder called Shil Kumar. The name “Jethwa” is said to come from a later chief, Jethaji. Bards added stories to these early generations over the centuries, so the oldest names are best read as tradition rather than record.

Ghumli, the old capital

Ghumli lies in the Barda hills, about 40 km from Porbandar. Before the Jethwas it was a town called Bhutambilika, capital of the Saindhava kings from the 8th to the 10th century. Under the Jethwas it became a fortified capital, and around 1220 Rana Shiyaji took the title “Rana of Ghumli”. The ruins that stand there today date from this time.

The Navlakha temple

Built in the 12th century in the Solanki style, often called the oldest Sun temple in Gujarat. Its name is said to come from its cost of nine lakh rupees.[1][2]

The Vikia Vav

A stepwell of about 60 by 40 metres, built by the Jethwa ruler Vikiaji and named after him. One of the oldest and largest in Gujarat.[2][12]

Vindhyavasini Mata

Her temple stands beside the stepwells, where the goddess is still worshipped today. Family record

Ashapura Mata

The temple on the hill above Ghumli.[2]

Legends and turning points

Every great family has its stories

Some are legend, some are history, and all of them are worth telling.

The beginning Legend

Born of Hanuman

When Hanuman-ji leapt across the ocean and set Lanka ablaze, he cooled himself in the sea. A drop of his sweat was swallowed by a great fish, and from it was born Makardhwaj. Father and son met only in battle, recognised each other, and embraced. Jethwa tradition names Makardhwaj as our first ancestor, and his grandson Jethi-dhwaj as the one who gave us our name. At Bet Dwarka, the Hanuman Dandi temple honours father and son together.[1][9]

1193 Tradition · date uncertain

The storm from the north

The clan’s traditional history says Morvi was lost to an army led by Qutb-ud-din Aibak, a general of Muhammad of Ghor. The Delhi Sultanate was founded later, in 1206, and Aibak’s recorded raid on Gujarat dates to 1197, so the year 1193 should be read as traditional. The clan turned west, to the Barda hills, and began again.[1][7][8]

1313 Legend & history

The night Ghumli fell

In 1309 the Jadeja chief Jam Unad came from Sindh and was driven back. Four years later his son Barmaniyaji returned. Rana Bhanji Jethwa was defeated, and the golden capital of Ghumli was left in ruins. Folk songs say the city fell under the curse of Son, a woman whose story is still sung across Saurashtra.[1][2]

1671 History

Porbandar won back

For generations the Ranas held on, first at Ranpur and then at Chhaya, through the age of the Gujarat Sultans and the Mughal empire. In 1671 Rana Vikmatji took the port of Porbandar back from Mughal control and built a fort there. Within fourteen years Porbandar was the Jethwa capital, and it remained so for more than 260 years.[1]

Every coronation Tradition

The blood tilak

The Mer community stood by the Jethwas in war and in peace. When a new Rana of Porbandar took the throne, the leader of the Mer would cut the tip of his little finger and mark the Rana’s forehead with a tilak of his own blood: a promise of loyalty, renewed with every generation.[1]

Chronology

From Ghumli to Porbandar

  1. 8th–10th c.
    Ghumli (Bhutambilika) is the capital of the Saindhava dynasty.[2]
  2. c. 1120–1150
    Sangaji is remembered as the first Jethwa chief to hold the title Rana.[1]
  3. 1193
    By the clan’s traditional history, Morvi is lost and the clan moves west to the Barda hills. Date uncertain[1][7][8]
  4. c. 1220
    Rana Shiyaji makes Ghumli the capital again. The temples and stepwells are built around this period.[1]
  5. 1313
    The Jadeja chief Barmaniyaji defeats Rana Bhanji Jethwa. Ghumli is left in ruins and Rana Bhanji moves to Ranpur.[1][2]
  6. 1500s–1600s
    The Jethwa seat moves from Ranpur to Chhaya, near the coast.[1]
  7. 1671
    Rana Vikmatji takes Porbandar back from Mughal control and builds a fort.[1]
  8. 1685
    The capital moves to Porbandar, the seat of the Jethwa Ranas until independence.[1]
  9. 1869
    Mahatma Gandhi is born in Porbandar. His father and grandfather served as Dewan to the Jethwa Rana.[3][13]
  10. 1923
    Porbandar’s merchant flag carries an image of Hanuman-ji, the clan’s chosen deity.[1]
  11. 1932
    Maharaja Rana Natwarsinhji leads India’s first official cricket tour of England.[6]
  12. 1947
    Maharaja Rana Natwarsinhji signs Porbandar into the Indian Union.[1][6]

Family record

Our family and the clan. The Jethwa name is carried by many families across Saurashtra, Kutch and the world. We share the clan name and its history, but no written record links our family to the royal line of Porbandar. Our own line is recorded from Dewji Bapa onwards, in the family tree.
